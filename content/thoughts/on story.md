This morning I gave myself the leisure to lay in bed and read "The Stranger," a work of Camus exploring the confrontation of man with the apparent absurdity of the world. Since I am not familiar with other's interpretation of the book, I found myself with the freedom to read it in a way I find interesting and relevant.

Upon finishing the final pages of the book, it seemed apparent to me who 'the stranger' was. Similarly to Jean-Paul Sartre's conception of 'the other' (the alienating presence of other people), the stranger was I, the one reading a story about the events in the life of another. Up until these pages, the book is written in such a way that the main character comes across as indifferent to the point of *lacking morality or humanity*. However, on the final page, he comes to a different conclusion than the reader.

> [!quote] I laid my heart open to the benign indifference of the universe. To feel it so like myself, indeed, so brotherly, made me realize that I'd been happy, and that I was happy still. (75)

In this sense of indifference the protagonist instead finds freedom and happiness. This is a much different value judgement than his prosecutor makes in the courtroom, or the reader makes from the distance of their armchair. For me, the main takeaway is not necessarily the absurdity and indifference of the world, which may very well be a valid realization, but the problem of communication. 

The protagonist's main fault lies in his inability to communicate with the world, to engage with it in story. He allows conclusions to be made about him in a courtroom that he feels excluded from, strangers to tell stories that portray him in this light or that light, and only begins to participate in them when driven to the point of indignation by a priest trying to ascribe religions meaning to his life, after which point this quote is taken. It is only in the final pages of the book that he begins to participate in forming a story about himself, to *realize* it in a certain light different than others. Where his own voice remakes indifference.
